# 👋 Hi, I'm Roy (Wenli Chen)

I'm an MSc candidate in Applied Psychology (Engineering Psychology) at Shaanxi Normal University. My research sits at the intersection of **human factors**, **human–computer interaction**, and **cognitive psychology**.

I currently study how pedestrians perceive, interpret, and respond to autonomous vehicles, using behavioral experiments, immersive VR, and synchronized physiological measures. In the longer term, I aim to apply psychological science to improve human performance, experience, and safety across human–machine systems.

---

## 🔬 Research Interests

- Human–autonomous vehicle interaction
- Pedestrian safety, comfort, trust, and decision-making
- Implicit and explicit communication in human–machine interaction
- VR-based experimental paradigms and human factors evaluation
- Physiological and behavioral measurement in HCI
- Age-friendly interaction and adaptive voice-assistant design

---

## 🧪 Current Research

- **Pedestrian-centered AV yielding:** Developing timing and distance thresholds for safe and comfortable vehicle yielding.
- **Right-of-way negotiation:** Designing socially responsive post-request feedback for AV–pedestrian interaction.
- **Shared-space interaction:** Studying how vehicle type, speed, and lateral distance shape perceived risk, supported by synchronized ECG and GSR.
- **Age-friendly voice assistants:** Modeling how age, speech rate, and task context influence comprehension and interaction satisfaction.
- **Auditory warnings in driving:** Evaluating the spatial design of in-vehicle warning sounds in a simulated driving environment.
